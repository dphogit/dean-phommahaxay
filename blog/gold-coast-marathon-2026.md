A couple weekends ago, I ran my second-ever marathon at the 2026 ASICS Gold
Coast Marathon event with a [chip time of 3:38:19](https://www.multisportaustralia.com.au/races/gold-coast-marathon-2026/events/1/results/individuals/8878/).

After a humbling first experience at the marathon distance last year at the
2025 Nike Melbourne Marathon, I realized that a few weeks of making up fitness
post-season from other sports would no longer do the job like it did for me in
half marathons.

For me, it meant parking amateur club football which I've played the last 15
odd years of my life, and opening a new door to the sport of running. This
year I've learned so much about how to train and prepare for races, and
believe I've setup myself up a good foundation to continue running long-term.

This post is a debrief on the race and my first official training block, as well
as some quick travelling tips.

The event didn't disappoint:

- A 6:15am start with ideal running conditions at a calm and cool 10-13°C
- A flat (~60m elevation gain), fast, AIMS-certified out-and-back course
- 15k runners pounding the pavement
- The half marathon is on the Saturday, and the marathon is on the Sunday, so
  you can still watch and support on the other day, or run both if you are crazy!
- A stacked elite field breaking records for both the half and the full
- Rob de Castella's famous pre-race speech
- Amazing crowds throughout the course
- A very well organized event and me wanting to come back in the future

## Race

I had 3 main goals for Gold Coast:

1. Get to the start-line healthy and prepared
2. Be happy with completing the race and how I finished
3. Run my goal time of sub 3:30

I achieved goals 1 and 2, and was 8 minutes off goal 3.

Split times:

| Distance (km) | Split | Race Time | Split Pace (min/km) |
| :------------ | :---: | :-------: | :-----------------: |
| 5             | 24:32 |  0:24:32  |        4:54         |
| 10            | 24:24 |  0:48:55  |        4:52         |
| 15            | 24:39 |  1:13:33  |        4:55         |
| 20            | 24:47 |  1:38:20  |        4:57         |
| 25            | 24:44 |  2:03:40  |        4:56         |
| 30            | 25:40 |  2:28:42  |        5:08         |
| 35            | 28:16 |  2:56:57  |        5:39         |
| 40            | 29:15 |  3:26:11  |        5:51         |
| Finish        | 12:08 |  3:38:19  |        5:32         |

As seen by the splits, I was one of many that was claimed by the classic
marathon wall. From 30k onwards I shuffled myself to each aid station and
took breaks by walking through each of them.

I managed to get a bit of second wind and found a steadier/slower rhythm
the last 3-4k and find my way home. I'm proud that I didn't let the wall
completely ruin my race, adapting with the aid-station strategy and salvaging
my B-goal of sub 3:40.

For fuelling, I took a 30g gel 15 mins before the race, and then tried to every
6km throughout the race, but ended up being every 7/8km in the second half.
I also took my planned salt capsules at the 50 minute, 1:50 and 2:50 marks
which helped because I did not cramp. This was another positive I took out
of the race.

Ultimately, I think I made a couple silly mistakes that cost me sub 3:30:

**1. Ran a tad to quick between 10-30k section of the race.** I stuck with the
3:30 pacer group for the first 10k which was reasonably quick at 4:45-4:55/km,
but I assume that was to get out of the crowd and get rhythm. I should have
eased back with them and sit between 4:55-5:05 once we hit the 10k. I
_disrespected the marathon_ as Rob de Castella says in his pre-race speech,
and should have saved myself until the 32k mark and gamble from there.

**2. Surging to catchup after a toilet stop.** At the 18k mark I had to make
a quick toilet stop losing me a minute. I tried to make this
up immediately within the km, getting my 18k split down to 5:20/km, but I think
this just burned through my glycogen stores - I should have steadily spread
out this minute recovery over a longer period and later in the race.

**3. Fitness and training mileage was just a bit short for 3:30.** While I
completed 3 30k+ long runs during my block, my weekly mileage for majority of
the marathon specific section of the block (weeks 15 onwards) was in the 60k
region, peaking between 65-70km a week. I think I could've reaped more fitness
benefits during the week as well, rather than my long runs taking such a large
percentage of my weekly mileage. I talk about this more in the
[training](#training) section.

I'll put these down to inexperience in the marathon and hopefully get better
with these over more races. A bit of perspective though, 3:38 is still a great
time that I am happy with considering it's my first year committing to running!

## Training

For Gold Coast, I set myself a 26 week plan to give myself time early-on to
get into consistent running and developing my base as it was my first
formal training block.

### Base Training

Before the 26 week block started in January, I spent time in November/December,
running around 5-10k 3-4 times a week to get some time on feet and getting used
to running frequently. I also did general weight/resistance training 1-2
times a week.

For the first half of the block (up to weeks 14/15), I was running 4 times a
week where I gently increased my mileage. My weekly structure looked like:

- Alternate between week types A and B.
- Week Type A: 2 easy runs, 1 quality session and 1 long run with race pace
  sections.
- Week Type B: 1 easy run, 2 quality sessions and 1 easy long run.
- Increased my long run distance 1k or 2k a week, starting from 13km.
- Every 4th week was a deload week, reducing my mileage before cycling again.

### B-Race: Auckland Waterfront Half Marathon

I had a checkpoint B-race at the flat and fast 2026 ASICS Auckland Waterfront
Half Marathon around week 14 or 15 of my block. I ran a 1:40:20 chip time.
The beginner gains were real, taking 8 minutes or so off my PB for the
half marathon.

I knew I was going to PB this B-race, my main goal was to get a baseline and
gauge a marathon goal-time. I arrived at the goal time of 3:30 by 1:40 x 2 +
10 minutes, as well as the [VDOT running calculator](https://vdoto2.com/calculator)
suggesting 3:28. In hindsight these ceilings I set were more on the stricter side.

Also was happy coming the half marathon out in one piece and with no problems.
I am looking forward to a half-marathon specific block in the future which I
know I'll be able to cut some time off.

### Build and Peak Phases

Because I ran the half-marathon what I considered conservatively, I only needed
a day off to recover and was back out easy running on the Tuesday.

I was still a bit worried 4 runs wouldn't do for the marathon, so bumped up
training to 5 runs a week:

- Weeks were structured with 1 quality session, 1 long run and 3 easy runs. The
  reduction in faster work was to accommodate for extra mileage and distances.
- Mileage peaked between 65-70km 3/4 weeks out from the block
- Practiced taking on nutrition (gels and salt tablets) on all my long runs.
- Followed a typical 3 week taper
- 3 of my long runs were over 30km:
  - 31km: 15k conversational, 16k race effort
  - 34km: all conversational
  - 31km: 6k conversational, 23k race effort, 2k conversational

Honestly besides the above I don't have anything interesting to talk about
the build and peak phases. I was just consistently stacking the kilometer's and
it all went by quickly.

## Next Block and Race

I was pretty proud of myself of accomplishing these distances in my
first training block, but I think there are some improvements to get that 3:30:

- Continuing to run 5 times a week, but incorporate two consistent quality
  sections within the week e.g. 1 workout during the week and 1 long run with
  paced sections, or 2 workouts during the week.

- More consistent mileage throughout the week, I think a 65-70km peak week was
  good for a first block. However considering 40-50% of these peak weeks were
  made of the gigantic 30km+ long run, there is room for improvement during
  the week. This could be simply increasing my easy runs from 40-55 mins (7-10km)
  to 60-75 minutes (10-12km).

- Practice being more aggressive with my fuelling during my long run marathon
  efforts. In practice long runs, I only fuelled every 7/8k (compared to the
  6k cadence I planned for race day) so I was naturally delaying taking on fuel
  in the second half of the race as my body wouldn't be able to get it down.
  I would also sometimes stop at the fountain midway through long-run efforts to
  take on this fuel. These minute or two breaks may have unintentionally
  given me unrealistic recovery time I wouldn't get during the race.

- Strength work. This dropped off from twice a week to none as I entered the
  second half of my block when I bumped up to 5 runs a week. I will commit to
  do once a week and stack it on a hard run or long run day, planning for the
  day after this to be a complete rest day.

Fortunately I've got a shot at redemption, having an entry to the 2026 Nike
Melbourne Marathon on the October 11th. I know this is a quick turnaround and
not ideal to do another marathon 3-4 months after, but life is short and
Kipchoge will be running so I can't miss out.

I have had a good full week off, and have reverse-tapered with a couple weeks
of easy running and park runs - getting my way back up to 35-40k. Next week I
begin my 10-11 weeks of the rebuild and will have another go at that sub 3:30!

## Travelling Guide

If you're like me who travelled to the Gold Coast for the event, here are some
things that may help with planning your trip. The event website also has
additional details which you can use to adjust your plans.

### Accommodation

I highly suggest to **start looking for accommodation once your entry has been
confirmed**, especially if you are travelling with others. The city gets busy
with the marathon, as well as it coinciding with school holidays.

Try to find a place that is walking distance from the [G:Link tram](https://ridetheg.com.au/).
This will help with you getting almost anywhere you need to in the main city of
Gold Coast. Each trip on the tram is only 50 cents, making it a cheap option
getting around!

### Bib Pick Up

The bib pick up and race expo is at the Gold Coast Convention and Exhibition
Centre, Broadbeach. The tram stops across the road outside (Broadbeach North
stop).

It will be less busy at the race expo on the Saturday as the half marathon is
on the Saturday. Otherwise, the Thursday and Friday may be busier as both
half marathon and marathon runners will both be there.

### Race Day

The precinct and race start is located at Broadwater Parklands, Marine Parade,
Southport. Again, trams run directly here giving you a couple stops which you
can walk from. The trams do get really busy before and after the race, some tips:

- If you pick your accommodation towards the end of the line, you will be one of
  the earlier people onto the tram.

- Trams start running from 4am, so I suggest try getting on as early as possible
  to avoid how busy it can get. It only took me 15 minutes going from Northcliffe
  (3rd/4th last South stop) to Southport station. You may want to practice
  catching the tram like you would on race day (e.g. watching the half marathon)
  to familiarize.

- It takes forever to leave the event on the tram if going south. It is already
  full and cannot load more people on when arriving. Take the opposite direction
  north and switch over at the north beginning/end of the line to come back south.
  Consider an Uber, your legs are already tired enough!
